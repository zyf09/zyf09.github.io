# Research Interests  
My research investigates the cognitive, computational, and neural foundations of social cognition, with a particular current focus on how people perceive and understand AI systems as social entities. I examine how people form impressions of artificial agents, infer their underlying capacities, and develop expectations about them. I am interested in how these judgments are shaped by AI designs, behavioral cues, prior beliefs, and social context.

This work builds on my background in social cognitive neuroscience. My previous research examined how self-related information is prioritized in cognition, the neural mechanisms underlying this prioritization, and how self-related biases are reflected in language. These investigations inform my broader interest in how people construct and update representations of themselves and other social entities.

I combine behavioral experiments, computational modeling, neuroimaging, and large-scale language analysis to connect social judgments with their underlying mechanisms. My broader goal is to understand how social cognition extends to artificial agents, using this emerging domain to test and advance psychological theories of how people understand themselves and others.


- ***Research Keywords***: Social Cognition, Human-AI Interaction, Self, Flow, Video Games
- ***Methods***: fMRI, DTI, TMS, EEG, fNIRS, Machine Learning, Reinforcement Learning, Drift Diffusion Modeling, Bibliometric Analysis
